## Reflection

### How do I interpret user needs and implement them into a program? How does creating user stories help with this?

I interpret user needs by first understanding what problem the user wants the program to solve. I then break those needs into smaller features that can be developed and tested. User stories help because they explain who needs a feature, what the feature should do, and why it is valuable. Acceptance criteria make the story more specific by describing what must happen for the feature to be considered complete. During the SNHU Travel project, user stories helped connect features such as destination recommendations and price filters to the needs of travelers.

### How do I approach developing programs? What Agile processes do I hope to incorporate into my future development work?

I approach developing programs by dividing the work into manageable pieces and completing one useful part at a time. I plan the feature, build it, test it, and use the results to decide what should be improved. In future projects, I want to use prioritized product backlogs, short Sprints, Sprint Reviews, and Retrospectives. These practices can help me find problems earlier and adjust the program when requirements change.

### What does it mean to be a good team member in software development?

A good team member communicates clearly, completes agreed-upon work, and tells the team when something is blocking progress. Team members should listen to different ideas, give respectful feedback, and share useful information. Being dependable also means focusing on the team’s overall goal and helping others when problems affect the project.
